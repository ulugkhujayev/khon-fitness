# Shoulder internal rotation stretch (shoulderir)

Note on side: app cues left arm, so stretch side is left. Review as left-side stretch.

## What this stretch targets

Primary: left infraspinatus and teres minor, left posterior deltoid, and posterior capsule. The behind-back position combines shoulder internal rotation with extension and adduction, which lengthens the posterior external rotators.
Secondary: left anterior deltoid, clavicular pectoralis major, and coracobrachialis get mild length from the extension component. Left elbow flexors hold the bent-arm position but are not the target.
Must contract or stabilise: left subscapularis, pectoralis major, latissimus dorsi, teres major, and anterior deltoid position the arm in internal rotation, adduction, and extension. Left middle and lower trapezius, serratus anterior, and rhomboids steady the scapula. Core holds the trunk upright. Right arm stays relaxed.

## Correct form, step by step

1. Set up: stand tall, feet hip width, weight even, chin level, shoulders level and down. Look forward.
2. Reach back: bend left elbow about 90 deg, bring left arm behind the back, elbow close to the side. Rest the back of the hand on the sacrum or lower lumbar area.
3. Slide up: let the hand glide up the spine toward T12 only until a mild pull without pain. Most internal rotation happens by the sacrum, and range above T12 adds little rotation.
4. Breathe and hold: breathe normally and ease farther on each exhale. Hold 15 to 30 seconds, relax about 30 seconds, repeat 2 to 4 times per side.

## Common form errors and how they change what gets stretched

Shrugging the left shoulder loads upper trapezius and levator scapulae instead of the posterior cuff.
Leaning forward, side bending, or twisting the trunk fakes hand height and moves load to the trunk and scapulothoracic joint, not the glenohumeral joint.
Arching the low back with flared ribs turns the move into lumbar extension.
Forcing the hand to mid back or higher jams the anterior shoulder and rotator cuff in a poor position and can cause front shoulder pain.
Letting the elbow wing far from the body reduces extension and adduction and weakens the intended posterior stretch.
Jutting the head forward, holding the breath, or bouncing causes guarding and blocks lengthening.

## Audit of the current highlight

Wrong: constant top and front left shoulder glow shows anterior and middle deltoid. Those fibers shorten in extension and internal rotation, so coral "stretched" color teaches the wrong target. Constant glow also hides progression from setup to stretch.
Highlight should change between steps: none at setup, light at first contact, stronger at end range.

Frame 0 stand: no coral glow. No stretch is applied yet.
Frame 32 reach back: light coral on left back shoulder below the scapular spine and upper back (infraspinatus and teres minor) plus left rear shoulder cap (posterior deltoid). No glow on left front shoulder, biceps, chest, neck, or right side.
Frame 63 slide up: stronger coral on the same left posterior regions as tension rises. Same no-glow regions as frame 32, plus no glow on top trapezius or anterior deltoid.

## Pose audit

Lower the left hand: start on sacrum or lower lumbar spine, end no higher than T12. Mid back is too high and forces compensation.
Keep the left elbow tucked near the side during reach and slide. Do not wing it out.
Keep shoulders level with the left shoulder down. Do not hike toward the ear.
Keep the chest square, ribs down, and spine tall at all frames. Do not lean, twist, arch, or side bend to gain height.
Keep the head neutral with chin level. Do not jut forward.
Keep the right arm relaxed at the side.
Show clear progression: neutral at frame 0, hand on sacrum at frame 32, slightly higher low-back hand at frame 63.
