# Cat cow (catcow)

## 1. What this stretch targets

Cat cow is bilateral and symmetric. Both sides work the same. There is no left side only target.

Cat phase (round, flexion): primary lengthened muscles are erector spinae (iliocostalis, longissimus, spinalis) in thoracic and lumbar regions, plus multifidus. Secondary areas are posterior neck extensors, rhomboids, and latissimus dorsi as the blades spread.

Cow phase (arch, extension): primary lengthened muscles are rectus abdominis and external and internal obliques. Secondary muscles are pectoralis major and minor, anterior intercostals, iliopsoas, and anterior neck flexors.

Muscles that must contract or stabilise: in cat, rectus abdominis, obliques, transverse abdominis, and gluteus maximus tilt the pelvis back and flex the spine, while serratus anterior spreads the blades. In cow, erector spinae, multifidus, and lower trapezius extend the spine and draw the blades together. Throughout, transverse abdominis, multifidus, pelvic floor, diaphragm, serratus anterior, and triceps hold tabletop.

## 2. Correct form, step by step

1. Tabletop: hands under shoulders, knees under hips, knees and feet hip width apart. Elbows soft, fingers spread, spine neutral, gaze down. Rest tops of feet flat on floor.
2. Cow: inhale. Lift tailbone, let belly lower, draw shoulder blades together, lift chest and gaze forward. Keep neck and low back long in a wide "U".
3. Cat: exhale. Tuck tailbone, round spine toward ceiling, tuck chin toward chest, draw belly button to spine, let shoulder blades move apart. Keep neck long.
4. Flow slowly between cat and cow for 5 to 10 reps. Hold each phase for one full breath, or 10 to 15 seconds for 5 reps. One physio source holds 15 to 30 seconds for 2 to 4 reps.

## 3. Common form errors and how they change what gets stretched

Rushing or rocking turns spinal motion into hip rocking. Forcing cow into deep arch loads joints and stops the abdominal stretch. Dropping the belly without lifting tailbone and chest leaves chest and hip flexors unstretched. Craning the head back compresses the neck. Forcing the chin down strains it. Hands forward of shoulders loads shoulders and limits spinal motion. Locked elbows or shrugged shoulders adds neck and wrist strain. Holding the breath removes the driver of the motion. A loose core loses pelvic control. Moving only the low back misses thoracic mobility.

## 4. Audit of the app's current cues against sources

1. "Start on hands and knees, with hands under shoulders and knees under hips." Verdict: keep. Suggested text: "Start on all fours. Hands under shoulders, knees under hips." (11 words)
2. "Push the floor away. Round your back and let your head drop." Verdict: reword. "Let your head drop" risks neck strain. Sources say tuck chin gently. Suggested text: "Exhale. Push floor away, round spine up, tuck chin gently." (11 words)
3. "Gently lower your belly and lift your chest. Move slowly between the two positions." Verdict: fix. It omits breath, tailbone, blades, and gaze. Suggested text: "Inhale. Lift tailbone, lower belly, open chest, gaze forward." (10 words)

## 5. Audit of the current highlight

Wrong: lower back and belly glow together in both phases, with no change. That mixes stretch and contraction. Coral means lengthened tissue, so front and back should alternate.

Highlight must change between steps. Suggested map:

- Frame 0 setup: light or no glow on mid trunk only, both sides. Do not glow belly wall, chest, neck, limbs, or feet.
- Frame 32 round: glow bilateral thoracic and lumbar erector spinae and between blades. Do not glow rectus abdominis, obliques, chest, neck, or belly.
- Frame 63 arch: glow bilateral rectus abdominis, external obliques, and pectoral chest. Mild iliopsoas glow is acceptable. Do not glow lumbar extensors or whole belly wall.

## 6. Pose audit

- Uncross ankles. Place feet hip width apart with tops flat and toes back.
- Check stacking: wrists under shoulders, knees under hips, gaze down, neck neutral.
- In round: tucked pelvis, high mid back curve, chin tucked, blades spread, arms straight without locked elbows.
- In arch: lifted tailbone, belly lowered without sagging, chest forward, blades together, gaze forward, neck long.
- Keep arms vertical. Do not let hands drift forward or elbows bow out.
- Move the whole spine. Avoid hinging only at the low back.
